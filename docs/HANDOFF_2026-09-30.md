Mechanism (supervisor's arithmetic, confirmed on the counters): `FeKick` polls with a 200 us sleep
(`fable2_p2_census.cpp` ~971) that lands at ~1.5 ms on Windows (the 0.5 ms timer floor plus scheduling); 9-11 waits
per frame make 15-18 ms of wait per frame; at 3x the GPU wall (~20 ms) hid it, at 2x it IS the wall. The recorder only
needs 0.62-0.73 ms of each wait (recorder busy / waits), 0.30 ms per batch with ~2.3 batches outstanding per wait.
GPU TIME is 10.8-11.3 ms/frame on this route at 2x, the hard floor. Pending max is 5 only in the load window; steady
windows 0 (the queue really drains to empty at every kick under setting 1).

## 4. The flash: it fires at night, and the "particles" were the flash

- Retrospective: dusk falls at ~0.85 of every standard capture (sky p95 by tenth is the same curve in every leg, ~100
  -> 40-46; f4916_full of V2X_OFF_2 and AR_3's f5450 are night scenes by eye). Binning the dirty 3x legs' events by
  tenth: AR_3 104/260 in tenths 9-10 (of 2,295), RL_2 70/50, AR_19 80/53, AR_7 49/53 - the untreated night tail runs at
  60-110% of the daylight rate. The three treated legs that set the 1.3.7 bar (RL_3, TH_2, TH_4b) carried the same tail
  and read 0 there: the pacing suppressed the flash at night in the regime where it was measured.
- CORRECTION (02:45). Small violet glows in the 2x legs were classified "game particles" by eye for two hours (a
  "sparkle cluster" by a lantern at the right edge, "wisps" among distant trunks). Two tests on the same files say they
  are the flash: every one lasted exactly one capture frame and its spot never lit again on later passes of the pan
  (right-edge region lit in 6 of 5,158 frames, 14 of 5,399, 2 of 5,185, all runs of 1; an emitter would light most
  frames of every pass), and known real small flashes at full resolution (RL_2 f0013/f0059) look the same - soft violet
  glows, some with a bright core - with `cardcorr.py` giving the same structure correlation (+0.62 vs +0.65). Four
  separators were tried and rejected on their positive controls: duration, shape, co-occurrence, per-blob exchange at
  capture resolution (the picture-level exchange from AR_3 stands). Memory: `a-one-frame-glow-is-the-flash.md`.
- Corrected 2x ledger (flash-coloured events per ~5,000-frame leg, the last two tenths at night):
  setting 1: ON_1 0, ON_2 0, NIGHT_1 0, NIGHT_2 0, NIGHT_3 0, W2X_BRIDGE 0 (six legs, ~12.8 min of night at 0);
  pacing off: OFF_1 0, OFF_2 4 (night tail); setting 2: P2_1 11, P2_2 1; provocative unpaced: D2X_DAY_1 5, D2X_NIGHT_1
  0; candidate: W2X_CV_1 0, W2X_CV_2 0. The route provokes weakly at 2x when the pacing is off or loosened; setting 1 is
  the only arm at zero everywhere; the user's night sighting at 2x with setting 1 is the one observation not reproduced.
- Night on this save: minutes ~5-12 of the game clock (a Fable II day ~20 real minutes); `uwnight2.sh` with a 240 s
  capture delay lands inside it (NIGHT_2/3: brightness 27/27/27, sky 38-39). The night subject rule: motion > 2
  primary, luminance < 150 ceiling, foliage advisory (`legsum.py` prints it).

## 5. The capture's blind spot (affects the whole corpus)

`burst3.ps1` reads `PrimaryScreen.Bounds` from a non-DPI-aware PowerShell: 3072x1280 on the 3840x1600 display at 125%
scaling. Every capture in the corpus covered the top-left 80% x 80% of the picture (f4916_full: the hero at 62% of the
frame's width, the region label cut). Leg-to-leg comparisons hold; absolute rates are per four-fifths of a frame.
`burst4.ps1` = burst3 + `SetProcessDPIAware` (prints "capture bounds 3840x1600"); tonight's queue kept burst3 for
consistency; `playcap.sh TAG SECONDS` (the user's own play, no launch, no claim) uses burst4. The next corpus needs a
new baseline before comparing with old counts.
